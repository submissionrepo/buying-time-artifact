## 0. Definitions

- **Release.** A unit of value leaving the bridge for good: an escrow release at its block time; an unbacked mint at its time for the part not later redeemed against the escrow; a redeemed part at its escrow-release time. Each unit is counted once.
- **First release (anchor).** The earliest time at which cumulative released value reaches 1% of the event's released value. Earlier, smaller transfers to the same recipients are reported as preliminary transfers (column `preliminary_transfers`) and do not move the anchor. Where per-release amounts are unpublished but times are known, the value enters as an interval between the first and last published times.
- **Main chain.** The chain carrying the largest released value; for an unbacked mint later redeemed against an escrow, the escrow chain.
- **Outflow duration.** Last unauthorized release on the main chain minus the main chain's own anchor (same 1% rule). **Time to 90%**: anchor to the time cumulative released value reaches 90%, computed only when the whole path is placed.
- **Detection lag.** Anchor to the operator's first public acknowledgment (X post time decoded from the status ID; blog, Discord, Telegram or status page otherwise). Where only a later statement is found, its lag is an upper bound (`detection_lag_bound_s`). **First alert**: the first public alert by anyone other than the operator, reported when it precedes the acknowledgment.
- **Pause lag.** Anchor to the time releases were stopped: an on-chain pause event or chain halt (last block before the gap), or a time stated by the operator or a post-mortem. Where only the announcement of a pause is dated, the lag is an upper bound (`pause_lag_bound_s`).
- **Failure class.** V: the parties or keys that authorize releases (validators, multisig or MPC signers, operator or admin keys, federation members) were controlled by an unauthorized party, so releases carried valid authorizations. D: release-side verification (destination contract, or the off-chain verifier that authorizes the release) accepted a message, proof, signature or event that did not correspond to a valid lock or burn, with no authorizing key under outside control. S: origin-side logic recorded a lock, deposit or burn that moved no value, and the release side honored it. O: other or unclear.
- **Release side.** escrow: value left a custody pool holding assets that back claims on another chain. mint: unbacked claims were issued (wrapped tokens, or release of a destination-side hub's pre-allocated native supply) and realized by sale, borrowing or transfer. both: an unbacked mint, then (partial) redemption against the escrow. other: neither.
- **Scope.** `bridge_custody_or_mint` (value left a bridge's escrow or a bridge issued unbacked claims); `bridge_liquidity_or_relayer` (a bridge's liquidity pool, or a relayer's or solver's own capital advanced for fills); `approval_outflow` (user wallets through token approvals granted to a bridge or aggregator contract); `not_a_bridge_failure` (hot wallets of an exchange or platform, a token contract, an impersonation site, a protocol without a bridge path); `operator_action` (value moved by the operator itself); `duplicate` (a second DefiLlama record of an event already listed). Vaults of cross-chain swap networks (THORChain, Maya) and router pools are coded `bridge_liquidity_or_relayer`. The **bridge set** used for all statistics is the first two scopes.
- **Amounts.** USD at the time of the event. Basis, in order of preference: our chain measurement of all legs at DefiLlama prices of the first release; documented released quantities times DefiLlama prices; the operator's figure; press or analyst consensus; DefiLlama. `amount_range` gives the spread across sources; `dl_amount_usd` keeps DefiLlama's figure.
- **Recovered** = value returned by the recipient, or seized and transferred to the operator, users or authorities. Value frozen in place (issuer or validator blacklists, governance freezes) is in `frozen_usd`. Operator reimbursement from its own funds is in the notes only. Empty means no source documents a recovery; 0 means a source states nothing was recovered.
- **Sources.** Each measured field has a source column marked `onchain` (block explorer pages or our chain data files), `postmortem` (the operator's own material: post-mortem, incident report, official post, repository) or `press` (news media, analysts, academic papers, data aggregators), with URLs.

  - E47 THORChain 2026: automatic solvency halt (signing and trading stop when more than 66% of nodes report a chain insolvent); outbound delay for large protocol-signed outbounds. ## 8. Caveats

- **Universe boundary.** The event list is DefiLlama's bridge flag and bridge classification plus four SoK incidents. Records DefiLlama files elsewhere are not included (for example outflows through approvals granted to cross-chain swap aggregators that it does not flag as bridge events, or token-supply incidents on chains with bridges).
- **Valuation.** Chain-measured amounts use DefiLlama prices at the first release, so they differ from headline figures valued at discovery (Ronin 2022: 540M at release-time prices versus 624M at discovery-date prices) or at later dates. Thinly traded tokens without a price count as zero (Nomad: 52 rows of one token; ZKSwap: 28 rows).
- **Release paths.** Inflows to recipient addresses (Mixin, THORChain 2026) count every transfer from the selected senders; for Mixin every sender to the two Ethereum recipients is counted, so a non-operator sender would inflate the total (the first 0.5 ETH transfer, from an unlabelled address, is treated as preliminary). Nomad releases are classified by whether the transaction entered a message receiver without a proof; the 29 proven messages in the window are excluded.
- **Detection.** Detection lags measure public acknowledgment, not internal detection; the SoK reports internal detection of 13 minutes (pNetwork 2021) and 6 days (Ronin 2022). Several operators first posted on Discord or Telegram, whose times are not always recoverable; X post times come from status IDs.
- **Pauses.** Most pause times in the distribution come from chain data (pause events, deactivations, receiver unenrollment, threshold changes, chain halts); stated times sometimes rest on one secondary source summarizing an operator's report (Wormhole 2022, Kelp 2026). Announcement times are upper bounds and are not mixed into the pause-lag distribution. For a few events a pause was impossible (immutable contracts: Aztec Connect, Aztec 2.0) or ended nothing because the custody was already empty.
- **Response delay** is the first documented response, so it is an upper bound on the true response time; internal responses (key rotation, private notification) are not visible in the sources.
- **Corrections to earlier material.** An earlier compilation's BNB release time (18:58:14 UTC) is not a hub release; the two releases were at 18:26:46 and 20:43:18 UTC (BSC logs). The first chain-data run for Nomad missed transactions through two of its message receivers (now 962 transactions, 186.8M). The earlier Orbit selection included a legitimate withdrawal and missed the first unauthorized release (corrected in the record).
- **Thin records.** The shared web-search budget ran out during the 2026-10-16 research; CrossCurve (E41), Hyperbridge (E44), Meter (E12), IoTeX (E42) and the Multichain router approvals (E09) have no release times, and several 2026 events lack an operator statement time. Fields stay empty rather than estimated.
- **Classification.** Failure class and release side are judgments from the cited descriptions; alternatives are given in the notes. Where the prior notes coded an event differently, the record says so.
- **Recent events.** Events after mid-2026 rest on sources published within weeks of the event; figures and timelines may be revised.
- **Time precision.** Block timestamps are to the second; a few start times are known only to the minute (marked in the notes).
